# Discovery/confirmation allocation does not repair remaining recovery

The objective is to retain independently supported CNA distinctions without
weakening confirmation or increasing unsupported subdivisions. This locked
development screen tests whether reallocating counts improves fitted membership
information. Neither unequal allocation warrants promotion.

## Model and decisive test

For a known negative-binomial shape r and mean m, beta-binomial thinning with
proposal fraction f produces independent NB marginals with means fm and (1−f)m
and shapes fr and (1−f)r. Consequently, the experimental exact-confirmation hook
transports both proposal expectation and shape by (1−f)/f. Transporting only the
expectation would change the observation model. This independence statement
does not automatically hold after fitting dispersion or other shared nuisances.

Three fixed discovery fractions, 1/3, 1/2 and 2/3, run all five original recovery
cases with pooled initialization and the exact-matched experimental path.
Original seeds, assertions, minimum support, posterior threshold and merge rules
remain unchanged. The half-share control must reproduce saved posterior arrays
and cell, segment, clone and difference tables exactly. More discovery counts
could sharpen memberships but reduce confirmation evidence; more confirmation
counts could strengthen evidence but degrade memberships. An allocation that
loses existing recoveries or fails to rescue the remaining case does not justify
a larger error/power experiment as a repair for this failure.

These are five selected synthetic regression cases, not independent biological
specimens or a false-split calibration panel. No population superiority or
equivalence is inferred from these counts.

Keep the half-share research control. Do not promote either unequal allocation
or run large null experiments for these nonbeneficial alternatives. The existing
998-fit pooled-initialization experiment retains its separately frozen half-share
design. Seed20261109 remains unresolved; more compute cannot justify overriding
its insufficient exact posterior support.
