## Thinning recovery

Three mechanisms were corrected while retaining the existing seeds, proposal
parameters, posterior cutoff, minimum interval length and scientific assertions.

1. **Mixture optimization.** Stopping at a tolerance proportional to the full
   likelihood made convergence depend on additive likelihood constants. The new
   optimizer uses a null-relative objective, analytic scores and local curvature
   checks. Unresolved fits are withheld. For the saved proposal, independent SciPy
   optimization gave gain 22.953964966189233; the candidate gave
   22.95396496618484, a difference of about 4.4e-12. The previous gain was
   22.1246987610. This certifies neither a global optimum nor a biological mixture.
2. **Interval support.** Uncertain flanks previously erased a qualifying interior.
   Difference intervals now consist of contiguous cores in which every bin passes
   the existing posterior cutoff and the original minimum length still holds.
   Low-support internal bins and chromosome resets cannot be bridged. This changes
   the selected interval set; it is not itself a specimen error-control procedure.
3. **Weighted count variance.** Depth matching forms `S = sum(w Y)`, whose
   conditional Poisson variance estimate is `Q = sum(w² Y)`. Treating S as an
   ordinary count overestimated that sampling variance. The corrected log-rate
   variance is `(Q + 0.5 c²)/(S + 0.5 c)²`, with `c = sum(w²)/sum(w)`.
   Counts, exposure and pseudocount now scale together when weights are rescaled.
   Unit weights reproduce the previous formula exactly. This remains an
   approximation conditional on fixed weights; NB dispersion, fitted matching and
   adaptive selection do not disappear through this correction.

## False distinctions: prespecified decision

The new global-rank prototype replays the complete **binned, known-reference**
search under the original matched-NB, continuous-state and state-plus-two-clone
generators. It does not replay public raw-feature/reference preprocessing, gene
vetoes or allele evidence. The frozen design uses 199 calibration specimens per
null law and 200 evaluation specimens per condition, with operating alpha 0.05.
There are nine simultaneous one-sided exact bounds: six null upper bounds and
three detection lower bounds, with Bonferroni family error 0.05.

The statistic is the maximum final pair score over eligible tumour groups.
Calibration and observed data use identical search/statistic implementations.
Rank guarantees require matching calibration/evaluation laws. Confidence bounds
condition on each realized calibration bank. A global rejection establishes
neither every pair nor any cell's CNA membership. Raw groups, raw membership ARI
and full membership abstention remain reported alongside the acceptance decision.
The target fails if its upper bound exceeds 5%; replicate counts and thresholds
will not be changed after seeing these results.

Job 22843218 completed all 998 fits. Each evaluation condition has 200 simulation
replicates. Bounds below belong to the single nine-member simultaneous family.

|---|---|---|---|
| Raw groups | 0/200, 2.56% | 184/200, 96.10% | 200/200, 97.44% |
| NB-calibrated global gate | 0/200, 2.56% | 184/200, 96.10% | 200/200, 97.44% |

**Decision: do not promote the gate.** The state-calibrated point error rate is
4%, but its upper bound exceeds the required 5%. Its detection rate is 32.5%.
Raw membership in the two-clone condition has median ARI 0.0764, unchanged by a
gate that accepts or abstains without replacing assignments. This experiment
supports the importance of observation-model misspecification and records the
cost of suppressing distinctions; it does not establish reliable clone recovery.
No alpha adjustment or extra evaluation replicates were added after these outcomes.

The retained original endpoint counts eligible tumour-group distinctions, excluding
the reference-like group. Reference-like assignments and raw ARI are also recorded;
this proxy is not the complete biological false-distinction event. The calibration
laws are supplied by the simulator, not independently established on specimens.
Neither this experiment nor more seeds supplies the missing biological null/power
panel. The package keeps candidate RNA groups and unvalidated status; the global
gate remains a diagnostic prototype with unsupported subdivision memberships.

## Uncertainty: target and conditional construction

The old coverage experiment tests the population systematic effect `mu_I = 0`.
Its observed mean log-count ratio is a statistic, not an unknown estimand. The
earlier wording naming that statistic an estimand is corrected here; the original
counts, six tests, coverage target and failed results are preserved. Routine
segment exports now identify the statistic and its center field separately, with
no validated parameter-interval estimand. The approximate SE and its unvalidated
flags remain explicit; it is not an interval around the HMM posterior effect.

The optional `haplodose.uncertainty.systematic_rna_intervals` helper requires an
explicit raw-count declaration and count provenance. Integer-valued transformed
inputs are ineligible. It assumes
`Y_j | Z ~ Poisson(E_j exp(mu_j + Z_j))`, `Z ~ N(0, Sigma)`, with independently
fixed E, independently known Sigma and an independently frozen bin/gap universe.
Its target is the unweighted interval mean of systematic mu, not realized latent
intensity or DNA copy number. Provenance strings record these requirements; they
cannot verify scientific independence. Fitted tumour residuals, fractional pools
and an expectation estimated from the same counts do not satisfy this contract.

Half the 5% error budget protects all bin intensities with
[Garwood Poisson limits](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/28.3-4.437); half protects
Gaussian latent means over every nonempty interval contained within a fixed
chromosome/gap block. Combining these events by a union bound protects arbitrary
subsequent HMM interval selection under the stated model. Zero counts correctly
allow an infinite lower endpoint. Negative computed covariance eigenvalues require
independent resolution; the helper performs no automatic covariance repair.

The procedure is not an automatic pipeline default. Its independent tests include
Poisson-CDF inversion at counts near 2,000, direct covariance quadratic forms and
exhaustive small interval families. The experiment retains the original six
coverage endpoints and evaluates legacy and new intervals on the same observations
and HMM selections. The separate power curve spans zero, half, one and two default
RNA gain-state separations. No universal power threshold is inferred from that grid.

Candidate 4 passed the independent numerical tests in the 309-test suite. The
subsequent raw-count declaration tightens the interface without changing interval
arithmetic. Job 22843743 completed the prespecified 5,400-fit experiment on the
verified CPU backend. The new method covered zero in 1000/1000 simulations at
each of six endpoints; each simultaneous two-sided coverage interval is
[99.4534%, 100%]. The separate legacy six-member Holm family rejected nominal
95% coverage in five conditions in this new replication, versus four in the
preserved earlier realization. The target and decision rules are unchanged.

Coverage comes with a substantial precision cost. At one default RNA gain-state
separation, `0.6 log(1.5) = 0.2433`, the prespecified six-bin interval excluded zero
in 159/200, 3/200 and 1/200 cases, respectively. Their nominal descriptive exact
95% intervals are 73.2–84.9%, 0.31–4.32% and 0.013–2.75%. At twice that effect,
detection was 200/200, 173/200 and 14/200. These are the specified E=2000-count,
120-bin, six-bin-signal regimes; the intervals are not a simultaneous power claim
over the operating curve. All widths were finite in this high-count experiment;
zero-count unbounded limits are checked separately in numerical tests.

**Decision: retain the helper only under its explicit model contract.** The
same-target construction resolves selection/dependence undercoverage in these
known-covariance experiments, with conservative bounds and often low detection.
It does not repair routine uncertainty when E or Sigma are estimated without
independent error control. Routine approximate SEs remain unvalidated diagnostics.
No covariance estimate, alpha allocation or effect grid was tuned to these outcomes.

The error budget is statistical; arithmetic comparisons and Monte Carlo intervals
are reported separately. Known covariance in a simulator is not evidence that the
required biological nuisance information has been independently measured.
